# Sesame-Seared Tuna with Snap Peas and Brown Rice

![Sesame-Seared Tuna with Snap Peas and Brown Rice](C:\Users\terja\Documents\AI_Projects\recipe-generator-fatty-liver-diabetes\data\generated_recipes\cookbook-recipes\Dinner\IMG\sesame-seared_tuna_with_snap_peas_and_brown_rice.png)

A steady supply of lean protein and dietary fiber helps maintain lasting energy throughout the busy week. This quick skillet dinner delivers a steakhouse-style seafood experience using accessible freezer staples.

## Details
- **Cook time:** 5–8 min
- **Prep time:** 10 min
- **Servings:** 2

## Ingredients
- 1 pouch (8.5 oz / 240 g) **Ready-to-eat cooked brown rice pouch**
- 8 oz (225 g) **Fresh or frozen tuna steaks (thawed)**
- 1 tbsp (9 g) **Sesame seeds**
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) **Extra-virgin olive oil**
- 2 medium (30 g) **Scallions**, sliced
- 8 oz (225 g) **Fresh sugar snap peas**, trimmed
- 1/2 cup (80 g) **Frozen shelled edamame**, thawed
- 1 tbsp (15 g) **Fresh ginger paste**
- 2 tsp (10 ml) **Low-sodium soy sauce**
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) **Unseasoned rice vinegar**

## Instructions
1. Microwave the brown rice pouch according to the package directions and set aside.
2. Pat the tuna steaks dry with a paper towel and press them firmly into the sesame seeds to coat both sides evenly.
3. Heat the extra-virgin olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Sear the tuna for 1 to 2 minutes per side, flipping halfway through, until the crust is toasted and the center remains pink, then transfer to a cutting board.
4. Reduce the burner heat to medium, then add the scallions, sugar snap peas, edamame, and ginger paste to the skillet.
5. Stir-fry for 3 to 4 minutes until the peas are bright green and crisp-tender, then remove from the heat and stir in the low-sodium soy sauce and rice vinegar.
6. Slice the seared tuna. Divide the warm brown rice between plates, top with the vegetable mixture, and arrange the tuna slices over the top.

## Variation
Swap the sugar snap peas for fresh or frozen broccoli florets.

## Storage
Best enjoyed right away; seared tuna does not reheat well.
